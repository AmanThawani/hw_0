The GPT-6 implementation is considerably simpler than mine, with fewer fields and less code, making it easier to read, understand, and implement. This aligns with my instruction to GPT-6 to prioritize simplicity. It also follows Django best practice by referencing settings.AUTH_USER_MODEL rather than importing User directly. Its core structure matches mine, as it uses separate one-to-one student and instructor profiles, unique enrollments, a random per-lecture QR identifier, an optional upload–lecture link, and an enrollment check in clean(). However, this simplicity costs completeness. The profiles store nothing beyond the user, and Course lacks a code, term, and year. So, offerings in different years are virtually indistinguishable. Also, PROTECT on the instructor forces past courses to be reassigned before an instructor can be deleted, while I use SET_NULL. Since GPT-6's Enrollment has no status field, in GPT-6's version dropping a course means deleting the record. Most importantly for cheating, in the GPT-6 version, Lecture has a date but no time, so the check-in window its own comments call for cannot be enforced. Also, QRCodeUpload blocks repeat submissions outright, discarding rejected attempts (which is suboptimal since these rejected attempts could later serve as evidence of cheating). Its ImageField also fails Django's system check without having Pillow installed. Overall, GPT-6 offers a minimal, accessible starting point, while my model needs more code but preserves history, supports analytics, and actually enforces its anti-cheating requirements.
